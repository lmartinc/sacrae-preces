Author Guide

Version: 1.0

Purpose

This guide defines the editorial standards for all Sacrae Preces publications. It exists to ensure consistency in style, tone, accuracy, and presentation across every volume in the series.

Every chapter, prayer, reflection, and explanatory note should follow these standards.

Mission

The purpose of Sacrae Preces is to help readers grow in prayer by presenting the rich traditions of the Catholic Church with clarity, beauty, and reverence.

The role of the author is not to introduce new teachings, but to faithfully present the Church's prayer tradition in a way that is accessible and spiritually enriching.

Intended Audience

The series is written for:

Catholics seeking to deepen their prayer life.
RCIA/OCIA participants and those exploring the Catholic faith.
Catechists and teachers.
Families praying together.
Young adults and older readers alike.
Anyone interested in Ecclesiastical Latin and Catholic spirituality.

No prior knowledge of Latin or Catholic theology should be assumed.

Tone of Writing

Every chapter should be:

Reverent.
Warm and welcoming.
Clear and approachable.
Encouraging rather than academic.
Faithful to Catholic teaching.
Suitable for readers of a wide range of ages.

Avoid language that is overly technical, argumentative, or sensational.

Accuracy

Accuracy is an act of reverence.

Before publication, every chapter should be verified against reliable Catholic sources.

Whenever possible, information should be supported by:

Sacred Scripture.
The Catechism of the Catholic Church.
Official liturgical texts.
Writings of the Church Fathers.
Doctors of the Church.
Papal documents where appropriate.

Where interpretation or devotional reflection is offered, it should be presented clearly as reflection rather than doctrine.

Standard Structure for Every Prayer

Unless there is a compelling editorial reason, each prayer should follow the same structure.

Half-title page.
English prayer.
Latin prayer.
Prayer at a Glance.
Place in Sacred Scripture.
Historical Background.
Why We Pray This Prayer.
Meaning of the Prayer (or individual petitions where appropriate).
Key Latin Words and Their Significance.
Traditional Times of Prayer.
Reflection.
Invitation to pray the prayer again slowly.
Reflections

Reflections are intended to help the reader pray more thoughtfully.

They should:

Encourage prayer.
Remain rooted in Scripture and Catholic tradition.
Avoid speculation.
Be approximately 250–500 words.
End gently without forcing a conclusion.

The purpose of a reflection is to accompany the reader, not to lecture them.

Historical Notes

Historical notes should:

Explain origins where known.
Distinguish historical fact from tradition.
Avoid unnecessary academic detail.
Remain concise and engaging.
Scripture

Scripture should always be presented respectfully.

Where appropriate:

Introduce the biblical setting.
Explain how the prayer relates to Scripture.
Encourage the reader to read the full passage.

Avoid isolating verses from their wider context.

Latin

Latin should always be presented in its traditional Ecclesiastical form.

Whenever practical:

Provide pronunciation guidance.
Explain important vocabulary.
Highlight recurring words that appear in multiple prayers.
Encourage gradual familiarity rather than formal language study.
Catechism References

References to the Catechism should:

Support understanding.
Never overwhelm the reader.
Be clearly cited.

The Catechism is used to illuminate the prayer, not replace it.

Saints and Church Fathers

When quoting saints or the Church Fathers:

Verify the quotation.
Provide context where necessary.
Avoid unattributed sayings commonly circulated online.
Visual Design

Every page should reflect the same principles as the writing.

Beauty should support prayer.

Avoid unnecessary decoration.

White space is intentional.

Typography should remain calm and dignified.

Artwork should always serve the text.

Editorial Principles

Every page should ask:

Is it accurate?
Is it faithful to Catholic teaching?
Is it understandable?
Does it encourage prayer?
Does it draw attention to Christ rather than the author?

If the answer to any of these questions is "no," the page should be revised.

The Rule of Sacrae Preces

We write with humility.

We write with accuracy.

We write with reverence.

We seek beauty without excess.

We distinguish clearly between doctrine, history, and devotional reflection.

We remember that our task is not merely to inform, but to help others pray.

Every page should lead the reader a little closer to Christ.
